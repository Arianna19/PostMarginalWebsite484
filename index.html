100-word summary

Project Summary

This project redesigns the Postmarginal website so visitors can understand the organization, discover people and projects, and move through resources with less friction. The work draws on accessibility conversations, mood boards, low fidelity layouts, component experiments, and two rounds of user feedback. Testing focused on the homepage, profiles, explore, resources, and about pages, revealing opportunities to improve navigation, content clarity, typography, and visual hierarchy. The final direction emphasizes plain language, predictable wayfinding, readable layouts, and flexible components that can grow with the project. The result is a public-facing digital experience that feels welcoming, organized, and easier to use for everyone.

Design outcome

What the Redesign Communicates

The redesigned experience is framed around clear entry points for a non-specialist audience: what Postmarginal is, who is involved, what visitors can explore, and where resources live.

01

Readable first impression

The homepage direction prioritizes immediate context, accessible language, and a stronger visual hierarchy so new visitors do not need insider knowledge to begin.

02

Predictable navigation

Navbar component work and variant states support clearer wayfinding between profiles, explore pages, resources, and about content.

03

Accessible presentation

Accessibility notes informed the mockup’s contrast, spacing, focus states, plain labels, and structure so the project can be understood by a broad public audience.

Process documentation

From Research to Iteration

  1. Research

    Accessibility meeting and transcript

    Notes from the TCCH UX Accessibility collaboration captured concerns, decisions, and next steps that shaped the redesign priorities.

  2. Exploration

    Mood boards and low-fi layouts

    The process included multiple mood board directions and low fidelity concepts before narrowing the visual and structural approach.

  3. System

    Reusable navigation component

    Component work focused on a single navbar with multiple states, making the site easier to scale while keeping interaction patterns consistent.

  4. Testing

    Feedback across core pages

    User testing reviewed the homepage, profiles, explore, resources, and about pages, then organized decisions, pending confirmations, action items, and next steps.
